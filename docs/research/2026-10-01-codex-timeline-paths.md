**Task 1 verdict: change.** The sequence works, but the English edition needs naming, Juliana’s question needs the novel inside the novel, and the android test needs empathy. The opening also exceeds four lines if the prompt counts as a line.

The factual checks:

- **Jung, 1949:** His foreword is dated Zurich, 1949. He asks the I Ching about his intention to introduce it to Western readers through the foreword. He gets **50, The Cauldron, with changing lines 2 and 3 → 35, Progress**. He then asks about his own action in the changed situation and gets **29, The Abysmal, changing line 3 → 48, The Well**. “Reads the reply as the book speaking” is fair: he explicitly personifies the book for the experiment. “About its new edition” is less precise than “about his foreword.” [Jung’s foreword](https://bathtubbulletin.com/foreword-to-i-ching-by-carl-jung/)
- **1950:** This is the first English edition of **Wilhelm’s translation, rendered into English by Cary F. Baynes**, not the first English I Ching. Legge’s English translation already existed in 1882. Turing’s paper appeared in October 1950. “Pass for a person in written conversation” is a fair short gloss, though it simplifies the paper’s initial man/woman imitation game. [1950 edition record](https://ofj.org/library/i-ching-or-book-of-changes-the-richard-wilhelm-translation-rendered-into-english-by-cary-f-baynes-foreward-by-c-g-jung/), [edition prefaces](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/572e7600b09f95a8f0e10244/t/58dba0e42e69cf36033d1c5d/1490788589499/Prefaces.pdf), [Turing’s paper](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/comp/50cog/readings/turing.html)
- **Dick, 1962:** At the end, Juliana asks why the I Ching wrote **The Grasshopper Lies Heavy**, the novel inside *The Man in the High Castle*, and what readers should learn. The answer is **61, Inner Truth**. Her interpretation belongs to the fiction. The original page can imply she asks why it wrote Dick’s outer novel. [Novel, chapter 15](https://www.modernlanguageexperiment.org/assets/philip_k_dick_-_the_man_in_the_high_castle.pdf), [publication dates](https://www.loa.org/books/252-four-novels-of-the-1960s/)
- **Dick, 1968:** The novel’s spelling is **Voigt-Kampff**. It measures involuntary physiological responses associated with empathy, using disturbing scenarios, often involving animals. Catching an android passing as human is its purpose; conversation or intelligence is not what it measures. [Novel excerpt](https://libropiuweb.mondadorieducation.it/media/libropiu/contenuti/120900036545_cattaneo_millenniumconcise/08_text_store/01_pdf/database/pdf/120_empathy.pdf), [publication date](https://www.loa.org/books/252-four-novels-of-the-1960s/)
- **Wang Bi:** “Read them as positions” is too narrow. His account connects lines with the situation, their positions, and their relations to other lines. The final page should state Six Lines’ intended method without promising that every reading meets it. [Wang Bi, *Explaining the Lines and Understanding Change*](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/周易略例/明爻通變), [your timeline map](/Users/auchan/projects/sixlines-shorts/docs/research/2026-10-01-timeline-brainstorm.md)
