# What makes the Nostromo's screens atmospheric

2026-09-27. The user rejected a HUD of boxed values, crosshairs and a title bar (b30f394, reverted
in e03c598) as "clutter without flavor". They described what they liked in Alien: "There was sound
effects, there was vector graphics, there was movement and panning and zoom. It was transporting
you into a specific view of the world, which is in the infographic. It was just very atmospheric."
They asked for a web search on essays about the Nostromo's screens.

## Findings

- **The boxes came last.** The screens were a few large vector drawings made at Syssim and the
  Atlas lab on an FR80 film plotter: the orbit simulation, and the terrain of LV-426 during the
  landing. Ridley Scott asked for something "busier", so Brian Wyvill added the message boxes
  ("DEORBITAL DESCENT", "SYSTEM :BL: 76.75 :OB:") around the orbit. The HUD we built copied this
  last addition and left out the main thing: the drawing itself.
- **The drawing is a place you move through.** Alan Sutcliffe's landing terrain is a landscape of
  ridge lines, drawn as the ship approaches and descends (14 pages of FORTRAN with FROLIC calls).
  The orbit is a globe of contour lines with the path round it. The view goes somewhere: approach,
  descent, landing.
- **Sound is machinery, not beeps.** For MU/TH/UR the sound designers avoided the usual computer
  beeps. They drew on old relays, printers and scanners, with some beeps from a Yamaha CS
  synthesiser. At the opening, Goldsmith's score plays for a few seconds, then gives way to the
  hum of the engines. For five minutes no one speaks. On the bridge, two computers seem to talk
  to each other (Scott called them Tom and Jerry). Jimmy Shields did the sound.
- **Slow movement through the space.** The opening is a long track through empty rooms. Things
  move inside the frame: drinking birds, fluttering paper, screens reflected in helmet visors.
  The frames are composed and the camera is slow.
- **The screen is a real object.** The screens are CRTs, curved and blurred, and sometimes the
  film cuts from a low-resolution screen to a sharper one. Alien: Isolation copied this. Its team
  recorded the game's screens onto VHS and played them on old tube TVs, with crushed cables and
  magnets to cause interference, then filmed the result. They held themselves to what could have
  been built in 1979. Its creative lead: "Lo-fi didn't necessarily mean crappy."
- **Dune's filmbook** (for the pacing): the images are distorted and hard to make out, and
  the pace is slow, so the viewer takes in the world.

## For the lesson

1. Make the hexagram a place the view moves through: approach it, go down to the line being
   discussed, circle a trigram. Our plot now only turns slowly in place.
2. Replace the single teletype tick with a bed of sound: a low hum under the music, relay clicks
   when a line is plotted, a printer's chatter while an answer types.
3. Film the screen as an object: a slight tube curve, bloom and a soft picture. A slow push in or
   pan across the monitor, never a shake (calm motion).
4. No boxes or furniture. Only the words the lesson needs.
