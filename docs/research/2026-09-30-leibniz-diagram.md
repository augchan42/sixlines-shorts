## 1. Bouvet's letter

**Date and place sent from.** The letter is headed "At Peking this 4th of November 1701" ([Bouvet, Letter I](https://archive.swarthmore.edu/leibniz-bouvet/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Letter-I-4-Nov.-1701-with-FOOTNOTES.web_2.pdf?__raw=1), edition reference AA I xx: N 318). It answers Leibniz's letter of 15 February 1701, in which Leibniz had explained his binary arithmetic to Bouvet (same source; also [Maitre 2022](https://scholarworks.calstate.edu/downloads/xk81jr681), p. 27).

**When Leibniz received it.** Leibniz himself wrote: "Your Reverence's letter dated Peking, the 4th November 1701 was delivered to me the 1st of April of the year 1703 by the care of the Very Reverend Father Gobien" ([Leibniz, Letter J](https://archive.swarthmore.edu/leibniz-bouvet/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Letter-J-18-May-1703-with-FOOTNOTES.web_2.pdf?__raw=1)). Maitre gives the same date ([Maitre 2022](https://scholarworks.calstate.edu/downloads/xk81jr681), p. 27).

**Where Leibniz received it: not stated in any source I found; most likely Berlin.**
- Leibniz wrote to Bignon from "Berlin, 7 April 1703", six days after receipt, saying "A letter from China provided me with it" ([Bignon letter](https://www.leibniz-translations.com/bignon1703)).
- The translators of Letter J note that Leibniz "returned to Hanover from Berlin in early June" 1703 ([Leibniz, Letter J](https://archive.swarthmore.edu/leibniz-bouvet/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Letter-J-18-May-1703-with-FOOTNOTES.web_2.pdf?__raw=1), note 39).
- One popular site says the letter reached him "in Hanover" ([ichingai.info](https://ichingai.info/en/learn/leibniz-i-ching/)). This conflicts with the Berlin evidence above and is a weak source. Do not say Hanover on screen.
- History of Information says the letter came "after a detour through England" ([historyofinformation.com](https://www.historyofinformation.com/detail.php?id=395)). I found no primary source for the England route. Unconfirmed.
- The gap was about 17 months. The ichingai.info figure of "nearly fourteen months" is wrong.

**What Bouvet said the diagram showed** ([Bouvet, Letter I](https://archive.swarthmore.edu/leibniz-bouvet/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Letter-I-4-Nov.-1701-with-FOOTNOTES.web_2.pdf?__raw=1)):
- Leibniz's number table "without changing anything in it, is the same as the system of the gua or small lines of Fuxi".
- He asks Leibniz to (1) extend the table to 63, "since 63 along with the zero which is at the beginning of the progression makes 64"; (2) change every zero into a broken line; (3) cut the table into two halves of 32 rows and bend each into a semicircle. Then "compare this model with the circular Chinese model which I am sending you".
- The inner square is "the second model which you see included in the first and which is a sort of magic square": the same 64 figures in different positions.
- In the square, the eight "classes" sit in eight horizontal rows of eight hexagrams. The first trigram "becomes the lower half" and the added trigram "becomes the superior half".
- He argues that binary "must not pass as a new science, at least in China", because Fuxi's system "seems to be none other than this [same] science".
- The letter is also full of Bouvet's Figurist theology: Fuxi as an ancient sage, a system "invented more than 4600 years ago", and Tai yi read as a sign of God.
- The translators' note states that the diagrams are "virtually universally attributed to Shao Yong 邵雍 (1011-1077)". The first place they appear in print is Zhu Xi's *Zhouyi benyi* 周易本義, where they are attributed to Shao Yong (Letter I, note 4).

**Archive and shelfmark.** Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz Bibliothek – Niedersächsische Landesbibliothek, Hannover, **shelfmark LBr 105, Bl. 27** ([Maitre 2022](https://scholarworks.calstate.edu/downloads/xk81jr681), Fig. 1 caption, also citing A I, 20, 556). "LBr" is the signature for Leibniz's correspondence at the GWLB ([GWLB, Leibniz-Nachlass](https://www.gwlb.de/leibniz/leibniz-nachlass-und-weitere-leibniz-materialien)). Commons names the holder as "Leibniz Archive, Niedersächsische Landesbibliothek" ([Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Diagram_of_I_Ching_hexagrams_owned_by_Gottfried_Wilhelm_Leibniz,_1701.jpg)).

**The handwritten numerals.**
- *Who wrote them:* "The Arabic numerals written on the diagram were added by Leibniz" ([Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Diagram_of_I_Ching_hexagrams_owned_by_Gottfried_Wilhelm_Leibniz,_1701.jpg), which cites Franklin Perkins, *Leibniz and China: A Commerce of Light*, Cambridge UP 2004, p. 117). I did not see Perkins's page itself. The Swarthmore translators describe the numerals without saying whose hand wrote them.

- *Yes, 0 to 63.* This matches Leibniz's own account of the order in Letter J (section 5).
- *Not confirmed:* whether the square also carries numerals. The sources speak only of the circle.

## 3. Leibniz's binary work before and after

**The 1679 manuscript.** *De Progressione Dyadica*, dated 15 March 1679, held in Hanover. It is the "earliest reliable document" on his binary system ([Bibnum, Yves Serra](http://www.bibnum.education.fr/sites/default/files/69-analysis-leibniz.pdf); [History of Information](https://www.historyofinformation.com/detail.php?id=395)). Strickland & Lewis list it as "On the Binary Progression (15/25 March 1679)". The double date is Julian/Gregorian ([Strickland & Lewis](https://www.leibniz-translations.com/leibnizonbinary)). Maitre also dates it 15 March 1679 ([Maitre 2022](https://scholarworks.calstate.edu/downloads/xk81jr681), p. 22). Strickland & Lewis also list a binary note from October 1674 and several from 1696–1701, all written before the diagram arrived.

**The paper he published after receiving the diagram.**
- *Title:* "Explication de l'Arithmétique binaire, qui se sert des seuls caractères 0 et 1, avec des remarques sur son utilité, et sur ce qu'elle donne le sens des anciennes figures Chinoises de Fohy" ([Maitre 2022](https://scholarworks.calstate.edu/downloads/xk81jr681), bibliography). In English: "Explanation of Binary Arithmetic, Which Uses Only the Characters 0 and 1, with Some Remarks on Its Usefulness, and on the Light It Throws on the Ancient Chinese Figures of Fuxi" ([Explanation](https://www.leibniz-translations.com/binary)).
- *Dated* 7 April 1703 ([Strickland & Lewis](https://www.leibniz-translations.com/leibnizonbinary)). It was sent to Bignon that day for the Academy's *Mémoires* ([Bignon letter](https://www.leibniz-translations.com/bignon1703)).
- *Journal, volume year, and when it came out:* *Histoire de l'Académie Royale des Sciences, Année MDCCIII, avec les Mémoires de Mathématique & de Physique*. Mémoires pp. 85–89, plus an unsigned account "Nouvelle arithmétique binaire" in the Histoire section, pp. 58–63. Printed in Paris by Boudot in **1705** (rare-book listings: [Biblio](https://www.biblio.com/book/novelle-arithmetique-binaire-explication-larithmetique-binaire/d/1384358064), [Milestones of Science](https://www.milestone-books.de/pages/books/003257/gottfried-wilhelm-leibniz/nouvelle-arithmetique-binaire-explication-de-larithmetique-binaire-qui-se-sert-des-seuls), [Peter Harrington](https://www.peterharrington.co.uk/explication-de-l-arithmetique-binaire-153285.html)). So the volume is for 1703 but it came out in 1705.
- *Disagreement:* Maitre writes that the paper "appeared" on 5 May 1703 in the "Journal de l'Académie royale des sciences" ([Maitre 2022](https://scholarworks.calstate.edu/downloads/xk81jr681), p. 27). That date may be when the Academy received or read the paper, not when it was printed. I could not open the volume on Gallica (captcha) to check. Safest wording: "published in the Academy's volume for 1703".

**What he claimed about Fuxi** (quotes from [Explanation](https://www.leibniz-translations.com/binary)):
- "this arithmetic by 0 and 1 is found to contain the mystery of the lines of an ancient King and philosopher named Fuxi, who is believed to have lived more than 4000 years ago".
- A whole line "means unity, or 1" and a broken line "means zero, or 0".
- "The Chinese lost the meaning of the Cova or Lineations of Fuxi, perhaps more than a thousand years ago … so that their true explanation now has to come from Europeans."
- Bouvet, once sent the binary method, recognised "that this was the key to the figures of Fuxi". The 64-figure diagram "leaves no further room to doubt the truth of our interpretation", so "this Father has deciphered the enigma of Fuxi".
- The agreement "leaves me with a high opinion of the depth of Fuxi's meditations".
- The printed paper shows only the eight trigrams ("the Figure of the Eight Cova"), not the 64-hexagram woodcut.
- *Date discrepancy:* in this translation the paper says Bouvet wrote "on 14 November 1701". The letter is dated 4 November. I can't tell whether the slip is Leibniz's, the printer's, or the translation's. Use 4 November.
- Leibniz does **not** claim here that he learned binary from Fuxi. He says he has "for many years used" base 2. In Letter J he adds: "had I not formulated my dyadics, I myself perhaps would have looked a long time at this system … without penetrating their significance. For much more than 20 years I have had in mind this arithmetic by 0 and 1" ([Leibniz, Letter J](https://archive.swarthmore.edu/leibniz-bouvet/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Letter-J-18-May-1703-with-FOOTNOTES.web_2.pdf?__raw=1)).

## 4. Shao Yong and the doubling method

**Dates.** 1011–1077 ([Bouvet, Letter I](https://archive.swarthmore.edu/leibniz-bouvet/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Letter-I-4-Nov.-1701-with-FOOTNOTES.web_2.pdf?__raw=1), note 4; [Maitre 2022](https://scholarworks.calstate.edu/downloads/xk81jr681); [Marshall](https://www.biroco.com/yijing/sequence.htm); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shao_Yong)). His courtesy name was Yaofu 堯夫 and his posthumous name Kangjie 康節. The Chinese texts below use both.

**Who called it 加一倍法 (the "add-one-double method", i.e. doubling).** It was Cheng Hao 程顥 (Mingdao 明道, Bochun 伯淳), not Zhu Xi.
- *Where it is recorded:* Xie Liangzuo's 謝良佐 *Shangcai yulu* 上蔡語錄, juan 3. Cheng Hao tried out Shao Yong's number method, found that it worked, and told him: 「堯夫之數只是加一倍法，以此知太玄都不濟事」 ("Yaofu's numbers are just the doubling method; by this I know the *Taixuan* is useless"). Shao Yong patted his back and said 「大哥，你怎恁他聰明」 ("Big brother, how are you so clever!") ([Wikisource, 上蔡語錄/卷三](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E4%B8%8A%E8%94%A1%E8%AA%9E%E9%8C%84/%E5%8D%B7%E4%B8%89)).
- The same remark is cited from *Henan Chengshi waishu* 河南程氏外書, juan 12 (*Er Cheng ji*, Zhonghua 2004, p. 428) by Li Zhen, *Zhongguo zhexue shi* 2021 no. 6 ([rujiazg.com](https://www.rujiazg.com/article/22305)).
- *Zhu Xi's comments:* Zhu Xi later described Shao Yong's method in the same terms. The *Zhuzi yulei* 朱子語類 juan 100 says of the *Huangji jingshi*: 「只是加一倍推將去」 ("it just pushes on by doubling"), and 「是一分為二，二分為四，四分為八，八分為十六，十六分為三十二」 ("one divides into two, two into four, four into eight, eight into sixteen, sixteen into thirty-two") ([Wikisource, 朱子語類/100](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E6%9C%B1%E5%AD%90%E8%AA%9E%E9%A1%9E/100)). The same juan also says 「《易》是卜筮之書，《皇極經世》是推步之書」 ("the *Yi* is a book of divination; the *Huangji jingshi* is a book of calculation").
- According to Li Zhen, Zhu Xi explained "doubling" as "one divides into two" in *Yixue qimeng* 易學啟蒙, juan 2 ([rujiazg.com](https://www.rujiazg.com/article/22305)).

**The stages.**
- The base text is the *Xici* 繫辭上: 「易有太極，是生兩儀，兩儀生四象，四象生八卦」. In English: the Changes has the Great Ultimate (太極 taiji), which produces the Two Modes (兩儀, yin and yang); these produce the Four Images (四象); these produce the Eight Trigrams (八卦) ([Wikisource, 易傳/繫辭上](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E6%98%93%E5%82%B3/%E7%B9%AB%E8%BE%AD%E4%B8%8A)).
- Shao Yong continued the halving past eight. The Swarthmore translators quote his *Huangji jingshi shu* 皇極經世書: "one divides into two, two divides into four, four into eight, eight into sixteen, sixteen into thirty-two, thirty-two into sixty-four … and thus the six positions of the Yi form their patterns" (SBBY ed. 7A.24b). A second passage reads: "One transformation yields two, two transformations yield four, and with three transformations the eight trigrams are formed … and with the sixth transformation the sixty-four hexagrams are completed" (7A.32b) ([Bouvet, Letter I](https://archive.swarthmore.edu/leibniz-bouvet/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Letter-I-4-Nov.-1701-with-FOOTNOTES.web_2.pdf?__raw=1), note 4).
- The same note says the diagrams themselves "are nowhere present in editions that preserve his original writing". They were probably added by a later commentator, and first appear in print in Zhu Xi's *Zhouyi benyi*.
- So each stage adds one line: 1 → 2 (one line) → 4 (two lines) → 8 (three lines, trigrams) → 16 → 32 → 64 (six lines, hexagrams).

## 5. The ordering, checked hexagram by hexagram

**Leibniz's own description** ([Leibniz, Letter J](https://archive.swarthmore.edu/leibniz-bouvet/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Letter-J-18-May-1703-with-FOOTNOTES.web_2.pdf?__raw=1)):
- "I begin in the circle with the bottom of the right side … with 0 and 1, or rather with 000000 and 000001. For it is necessary to look at the model as if the eye were located at the center, and to begin with the lines near the center."
- The count goes up the right side to the top ("011111 or else 31"). It then "start[s] again from the left side on the bottom … 100000 or 32", and goes up the left side to finish "with … 111111, or 63".
- He considers "the line closest to the center always being considered as the first", i.e. the leftmost digit.
- "With regard to the inscribed square, the order there is perfectly natural, beginning with 000000 000001 000010 000011 etc., 0 1 2 3."

**Which line is the most significant bit.** In the circle, the line nearest the centre is the hexagram's **bottom** line; the outermost line is the top (translators' note 3 in [Bouvet, Letter I](https://archive.swarthmore.edu/leibniz-bouvet/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Letter-I-4-Nov.-1701-with-FOOTNOTES.web_2.pdf?__raw=1)). Leibniz writes that line first. So **the bottom line is the most significant bit (worth 32) and the top line is the least significant (worth 1)**. Read the lines bottom to top and write them left to right, solid = 1 and broken = 0, and you get the binary numeral.
- Marshall confirms this from the sequence itself: "the top line alternates between yin and yang from hexagram to hexagram, the bottom line is yin for the first 32 hexagrams and then yang for the second 32" ([Marshall](https://www.biroco.com/yijing/sequence.htm)).
- *Warning, sources disagree:* many web pages get this backwards. Marshall shows that Richard Rutt's *Zhouyi* (p. 91) states it wrongly ([Marshall](https://www.biroco.com/yijing/sequence.htm)). Popular sites also say the top line is most significant, and one even gives Bo as 100000 (e.g. results surfaced from [masterseanchan.com](https://www.masterseanchan.com/iching-8-trigrams/) and [ichingai.info](https://ichingai.info/en/learn/leibniz-i-ching/)). The translators' note 3 describes the lines as "ordered from outer to inner, from top to bottom", which is confusing wording. Its worked examples (Bo = 000001, Bi = 000010) agree with the rule above.

**Worked check** (lines listed bottom to top, 1 = solid, 0 = broken):

| Hexagram | Lines (bottom→top) | Binary | Value | Where in the circle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 坤 Kun | 0 0 0 0 0 0 | 000000 | 0 | bottom of right half (start) |
| 剝 Bo | 0 0 0 0 0 1 (only top solid) | 000001 | 1 | next up the right half |
| 比 Bi | 0 0 0 0 1 0 (only 5th solid) | 000010 | 2 | third on the right half |
| 姤 Gou | 0 1 1 1 1 1 (only bottom broken) | 011111 | 31 | top of right half |
| 復 Fu | 1 0 0 0 0 0 (only bottom solid) | 100000 | 32 | bottom of left half |
| 夬 Guai | 1 1 1 1 1 0 (only top broken) | 111110 | 62 | next to last, left half |
| 乾 Qian | 1 1 1 1 1 1 | 111111 | 63 | top of left half (end) |

The positions of Kun, Bo, Bi, Gou, Fu and Qian match the translators' note 3 ([Bouvet, Letter I](https://archive.swarthmore.edu/leibniz-bouvet/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Letter-I-4-Nov.-1701-with-FOOTNOTES.web_2.pdf?__raw=1)): "Kun … at the bottom of the right half … Bo … upwards through to Da guo and finally Kou [Gou] at the top of the right half … Fu … Yi … Guai … and ending with … Qian at the top of the left half". The note also gives Bo = 1 and Bi = 2 explicitly. The line patterns are the standard ones (Bo = mountain over earth, Bi = water over earth, Fu = earth over thunder, Guai = lake over heaven, Gou = heaven over wind).

In the circle, Kun (0) sits at the bottom next to Fu (32), and Qian (63) sits at the top next to Gou (31). So the two halves are numbered in the same direction, bottom to top, and do not run all the way round. Leibniz noticed this: "instead of continuing in the circle … we start again from the left side on the bottom". He suggested it was so that 0 faces 32, 1 faces 33, and so on (Letter J).

**The inner 8×8 square.**
- *Rows by lower trigram, columns by upper trigram.* Bouvet: the eight classes sit "in as many horizontal lines containing eight sextilinear characters", with the first trigram as the lower half ([Bouvet, Letter I](https://archive.swarthmore.edu/leibniz-bouvet/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Letter-I-4-Nov.-1701-with-FOOTNOTES.web_2.pdf?__raw=1)). Marshall: "all 8 hexagrams in the uppermost horizontal octet have kun as the lower trigram" and "the upper trigram in each column of 8 hexagrams [is] the same". The column order left to right is kun, gen, kan, xun, zhen, li, dui, qian. Kun is top-left (000000) and Qian bottom-right (111111) ([Marshall](https://www.biroco.com/yijing/sequence.htm)).
- Trigram values, bottom line as the high bit: Kun 000 = 0, Gen 001 = 1, Kan 010 = 2, Xun 011 = 3, Zhen 100 = 4, Li 101 = 5, Dui 110 = 6, Qian 111 = 7. So **hexagram value = 8 × lower trigram + upper trigram**. The row gives the lower trigram and the column gives the upper one. Reading the square like a page gives 0, 1, 2 … 63, which fits Leibniz's "perfectly natural" order.
- Check: Bo = lower Kun (0), upper Gen (1) → 1, first row, second column ✓. Bi = lower Kun, upper Kan (2) → 2 ✓. Fu = lower Zhen (4), upper Kun (0) → 32, first cell of row 5 ✓. Guai = lower Qian (7), upper Dui (6) → 62 ✓. Qian = 63, last cell ✓.
- *Not checked against the woodcut itself:* which corner Kun occupies on the actual print. I did not view or download the image. Marshall's layout is the standard one. Leibniz's statement that the square begins with 000000 agrees with it but does not name a corner.

**The Chinese count runs the other way.** Traditional numbering starts from Qian: 「乾一、兌二、離三、震四、巽五、坎六、艮七、坤八」 ([Wikisource, 先天圖論](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%85%88%E5%A4%A9%E5%9C%96%E8%AB%96)). Leibniz starts from Kun = 0 because he made yin = 0. This is part of what Maitre calls the "'wrong' reading direction used by Bouvet and Leibniz—opposite to the one used in China" ([Maitre 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40827050/)).

## 6. Is the I Ching "binary"? How historians judge it

**Leibniz did not learn binary from the diagram.** He had it by 1679 (*De Progressione Dyadica*, section 3). The diagram reached him in 1703. He himself said he had the arithmetic "for much more than 20 years" and would not have understood the gua without it ([Leibniz, Letter J](https://archive.swarthmore.edu/leibniz-bouvet/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Letter-J-18-May-1703-with-FOOTNOTES.web_2.pdf?__raw=1)).
- Maitre concludes that "Leibniz developed his dyadic independently of the Xiantiantu". The diagram's only effect was on the publication of the *Explication* in 1703 ([Maitre 2022](https://scholarworks.calstate.edu/downloads/xk81jr681), abstract and conclusion).
- *Minority view:* Hu Yang and Li Zhangduo (2006) argue that Leibniz could have seen a version of the square before 1679. Martino Martini's *Sinicae historiae decas prima* (1658) printed it "flipped". Maitre treats this as the strongest argument for influence, then rejects it ([Maitre 2022](https://scholarworks.calstate.edu/downloads/xk81jr681), pp. 22–23).
- English Wikipedia says Shao Yong's sequence "influenced Leibniz" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shao_Yong)). Given the dates, that wording is not supported.

**He read it as confirmation, and as a tool for the mission.** He treated it as "the key to the figures of Fuxi" and "the most ancient monument of science" ([Explanation](https://www.leibniz-translations.com/binary)). He hoped it would impress the Kangxi emperor ([Leibniz, Letter J](https://archive.swarthmore.edu/leibniz-bouvet/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Letter-J-18-May-1703-with-FOOTNOTES.web_2.pdf?__raw=1)). Strickland notes that Bouvet had suggested, "erroneously as it happens", that binary was the key to the hexagrams ([Bignon letter](https://www.leibniz-translations.com/bignon1703), note 3).

**What Shao Yong did and did not do.**
- *Did:* he produced an *ordering* of the 64 hexagrams by repeated halving (section 4). That ordering is the same sequence as counting 0–63 in base 2.
- *Did not:* he did not treat the hexagrams as numerals to add or multiply. The Swarthmore translators write that he was "not at all intimating a binary numerology". He compared the halving to how ten becomes a hundred and a hundred a thousand ([Bouvet, Letter I](https://archive.swarthmore.edu/leibniz-bouvet/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Letter-I-4-Nov.-1701-with-FOOTNOTES.web_2.pdf?__raw=1), note 4).
- Zhu Xi called Shao Yong's book one of calculation (推步), "a book not related to the *Yi*" (「其書與易自不相干」) ([Wikisource, 朱子語類/100](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E6%9C%B1%E5%AD%90%E8%AA%9E%E9%A1%9E/100)).
- Huang Zongxi 黃宗羲's *Yixue xiangshu lun* 易學象數論 goes further: 「康節加一倍之法，從此章而得，實非此章之旨」 ("Kangjie's doubling method was drawn from this chapter [the *Xici*] but is really not its meaning"). The 16- and 32-stages were Shao Yong's own additions ([Wikisource, 易學象數論/先天圖一](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E6%98%93%E5%AD%B8%E8%B1%A1%E6%95%B8%E8%AB%96/%E5%85%88%E5%A4%A9%E5%9C%96%E4%B8%80)).

**Where scholars disagree.** Maitre (2025) argues that the diagram *is* binary in structure, because it is "constituted of two elements and the binary system can use other symbols than 0 and 1". She reports that "some scholars argue that the diagram is not binary because of the different construction of the two systems" (division vs. multiplication) and the reversed reading direction ([Maitre 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40827050/)). The fair summary is:
- The arrangement matches binary counting.
- The Chinese tradition built it by halving, did not calculate with it, and counted it from Qian, not from zero.
- "The I Ching invented binary" and "Leibniz got binary from the I Ching" are both wrong.

## 7. Shao Yong vs Wang Bi

- **Shao Yong: image-and-number (象數).** He followed "the Xiangshu Study (image-number study), an approach based on iconographic and cosmological concepts" ([Maitre 2022](https://scholarworks.calstate.edu/downloads/xk81jr681), note 3). Wikipedia says the same: "xiangshu xue (象數學, 'image-number study')" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shao_Yong)).
- **Wang Bi (226–249): away from image-number readings.** The dates are from [IEP, "Wang Bi"](https://iep.utm.edu/wangbi/) (Ronnie Littlejohn) and [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Bi). The IEP says he set aside "an over-reliance on mathematical and symbolic readings of the text (typical of Han scholars)".
- The key text is his *Zhouyi lüeli* 周易略例, "Ming xiang" 明象 ([Wikisource](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%91%A8%E6%98%93%E7%95%A5%E4%BE%8B/%E6%98%8E%E8%B1%A1)):
  - 「言者所以明象，得象而忘言。象者所以存意，得意而忘象。」 "Words are for making the image clear; once you have the image, forget the words. The image is for holding the meaning; once you have the meaning, forget the image."
  - 「得意在忘象，得象在忘言」 "Getting the meaning lies in forgetting the image."
  - 「義茍在健，何必馬乎？類茍在順，何必牛乎？」 "If the sense is strength, why must it be a horse? If the kind is compliance, why must it be an ox?"
  - He attacks the Han image-number methods by name: 「互體不足，遂及卦變，變又不足，推致五行」 "when interlocking trigrams were not enough, they went on to hexagram changes; when changes were not enough, they pushed on to the Five Phases". He calls the result 「存象忘意」, "keeping the image and forgetting the meaning".
- So the contrast holds. Wang Bi reads the hexagrams for their meaning and treats images as disposable. Shao Yong's diagram belongs to the image-number line that Wang Bi argued against.
