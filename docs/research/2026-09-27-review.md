## What the store copy says the lessons are for

From the App Store description:

- "an I-Ching for people who would rather sharpen their judgment than borrow another answer"
- "It gives you a situation in compressed form: positions, tensions, changes, warnings,
  openings. It rarely resolves into a single clean answer."
- "The machine reasons across the structure and stops short of drawing your conclusion."
- "It looks for what the text withholds: a favourable reading can carry a warning, and a hard
  one can hold the way through."
- "don't ask only why you should believe something. Ask what would make you stop."
- "You'll know Six Lines worked when you close it, notice the story you were about to tell
  yourself, and don't cast at all."

Held against our copy:

- **A lesson stops short.** The best-scoring lessons already do: "Did nothing wrong, still
  lost the ox" is a situation with its point, and each short ends on a question. The ones that
  fail read as an order or a verdict ("Make the penalties clear to all"). Add it to the copy
  rules in the lesson notes: a lesson shows the situation and stops; it does not tell the
  viewer what to do.
- **"What the text withholds" is the lesson kind we use least.** The lesson notes found that a
  sentence which turns the short's point (a cost, a limit, a surprise) scored higher than one
  that agrees with it. That is the store's "a favourable reading can carry a warning". Use it
  first in any rewrite.
- **The Wang Bi lesson never says why the structure matters.** It teaches places, centres,
  answering lines and the master, and stops. One line on the last page, in the store's words:
  the readout is the structure; the conclusion is yours. That is the answer to a viewer who
  asks why they should learn any of it.
- **The shorts already fit the last line.** They end on a question and show no app screen;
  nothing asks the viewer to cast.

## Recommendations

Each is a decision for the user. Ordered by what I would do first.

### 1. Put the flight look on the full Wang Bi lesson, with one code change first

The flight look was built and judged on a single page. On the full lesson every page that
shows a hexagram would fly in again: `flightCam` starts each page at distance 70, azimuth
-40, and comes in over `approach + 2` seconds (approach defaults to 0), so 18 pages would
each open with a 2 s fly-in and a jump of the view at each cut. That is the opposite of calm
motion.

Change: the camera is one state (distance, azimuth, elevation, target) carried across pages.
It moves only on a page that sets `approach` or `fly`: one approach at the first look at 22
(chapter 2), one descent to L5 in the Wang Bi chapter, then it stays down for the rest of
22's pages, and one new approach when chapter 8 cuts to hexagram 7. The slow orbit stays,
since it is what makes the screen a view and not a diagram.

Sound follows the camera: the sweep on each of the two approaches, chatter while the lines
plot, the warble on the descent, the wind-down on the lesson's last answer, the beacon
through the approach pages, the music only on the end card. The four cues stay four; the boot
inventory has 13 kinds and adding more would be clutter.

The last page gets the one line from the store copy (above).

**Render:** the full lesson once (about 3.3 min with the end card). This is the one render I
think is needed. Items 2 and 3 ride on it.

### 2. Offer the beacon at the level of the engine

The beacon has been in since round 7 at `mix.beacon 1`, where it measures 4 blips a minute on
the render; the landing reference has its beep level with the engine and it registers at 40.
Blips are the chirp metric and the sound doc's target for the bed is "a few, quiet", so the
aim is not a count: it is the beacon sitting at the bed's level (about 0 dB against it, as in
the landing), one low pitch on a steady pulse, only on approach pages. The full-lesson render
carries it there, with a note that it is louder than round 10; the round 10 level is the
fallback if it is too much.

### 3. Two-pass loudnorm, checked

`explainer.mjs` runs `loudnorm=I=-16:TP=-1.5` in one pass with no measured values, which is
ffmpeg's dynamic mode: it adjusts gain as it goes, against the jump we built (the music 8 dB
above the lesson on the cut). Measure first, then apply the measured values with
`linear=true`, so the whole file shifts by one gain and the jump is kept. Caveat: ffmpeg falls
back to dynamic mode when the linear gain would push a peak past TP; the music section is the
loud part, so this is likely. Check `normalization_type` in the second pass's JSON; if it fell
back, lower I one step or limit the peaks before loudnorm. Folded into the same render;
verified by measuring the lesson and end-card sections separately after.

### 4. The end card at 0.75 speed: re-render the clip if it stutters

`playbackRate 0.75` on a 30 fps clip repeats one frame in four. The clips are cut to the beat
(22's is `join-101001-91.99bpm-9b`, pick14's tempo), and 0.75 × 91.99 is off pick13's 89 bpm
grid anyway. If the user sees a stutter on round 10, the fix is a Blender re-render of 22's
join card for the lesson at pick13's tempo, 9 beats at 89 bpm (about 6.1 s; or 12 beats,
8.1 s, for the 7.8 s hold the user asked for), so the frames are real and the card lands on
the beat. About 2:17 of Blender. If nothing shows, leave it. No render until the user has seen
round 10.

### 5. The period: decide whether it applies to all 64 shorts

The user removed the period from 22's card, which the lesson uses. The other 63 clips the

## What I would not change

- **Two products, two looks.** The shorts are 30 s, whip cuts on the beat, the hexagram build
  and one sentence. The lesson is a 3-minute terminal inquiry with machine sounds. Keep the
  flight look and the machine sounds out of the shorts; keep the whip cuts out of the lesson.
- **The lesson's colour rule.** Green frame, amber for the line to look at, cyan for the
  prompt, blue only to explain a point. The flight test keeps it. Apply it as is to the full
  lesson.
- **The bed.** The user liked it from round 7 on. On the render it passes the first targets
  (sharpness 1.37 against ≤ 1.6; roughness 0.06; 1.6 % at 2–6 kHz on a phone; 4 blips a
  minute, all the beacon) and not the tightened one (sharpness under 1.0: the Nostromo beds
  measure 0.4–1.0, the boot sequence 1.73). Leave it until the user asks for darker.
- **Copy.** The critic-passed readout, picture and character lessons. The lesson notes' rules
  hold (a thing happening, not a principle; clear to a newcomer; not the short's own point
  again), with the store's addition above: show the situation and stop.
