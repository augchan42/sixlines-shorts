# Fact check: Wuyue / Leifeng Pagoda / Baoqieyin dharani stupa

Short neon history video on the Wuyue kingdom (907–978, Hangzhou). Sources checked 2026-09-29.

---

## 1. Qian Chu printed the Baoqieyin Dharani sutra; copies dated 956, 965, 975 survive

**Verdict: confirmed**

Qian Chu (錢俶 / 钱俶, King of Wuyue) sponsored three dated printings of the *一切如來心秘密全身舍利寶篋印陀羅尼經* (Sarvatathāgatādhiṣṭhānahṛdayaguhyadhātukaraṇḍamudrā-dhāraṇī, "Dharani of the Secret Whole-Body Relic Casket Seal of the Heart of All Tathagatas"): 956, 965, and 975. The 956 and 965 printings were made for miniature bronze and iron stupas respectively; the 975 printing was made for the Leifeng Pagoda itself, then under construction. Copies of the 975 print were found among Leifeng's bricks after its 1924 collapse.

Recommended wording:
> Qian Chu had the Baoqieyin Dharani sutra (一切如來心秘密全身舍利寶篋印陀羅尼經, "Dharani of the Secret Whole-Body Relic Casket Seal") printed three times — in 956, 965 and 975. Copies of all three survive.

Sources:
- Jinah Kim / Aurora Gómez, "What Was in the 'Precious Casket Seal'? Material Culture of the Karaṇḍamudrā Dhāraṇī throughout Medieval Maritime Asia," *Religions* 12(1):13 (2021), https://doi.org/10.3390/rel12010013
- Baidu Baike, 一切如来心秘密全身舍利宝箧印陀罗尼经, https://baike.baidu.com/item/一切如来心秘密全身舍利宝箧印陀罗尼经/8855406

## 2. The sutra teaches that a stupa holding the dharani becomes a reliquary of all Tathagatas

**Verdict: confirmed, needs plain paraphrase**

The sutra (whose English title is literally "The Whole-Body Relic Treasure Chest Seal Dharani") states that placing a copy of the dharani inside a stupa transforms that stupa — however modest its material — into the equivalent of a reliquary holding the relics ("whole body," 全身舍利) of all Buddhas. Chinese text: *若人作塔以土石木金銀銅鉛，書此神咒安置其中，才安置已，其塔即為七寶所成。*

Recommended one-line paraphrase:
> Put a copy of this dharani inside any stupa, and the sutra says that stupa becomes a reliquary holding the relics of every Buddha.

Sources:
- The Sutra of the Whole-Body Relic Treasure Chest Seal Dharani (translation), http://www.buddhism.org/Sutras/3/sutra03.html and https://archive.org/details/TheSutraOfTheWholeBodyRelicTreasureChestSealDharani
- Zh.Wikisource, 一切如來心秘密全身舍利寶篋印陀羅尼經, https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hans/一切如來心秘密全身舍利寶篋印陀羅尼經
- Kim/Gómez, *Religions* 12(1):13 (2021), https://doi.org/10.3390/rel12010013

## 3. "84,000 stupas, each with a copy" — small metal stupas made by Qian Chu with the printed dharani inside; which dated prints came from which container

**Verdict: needs rewording**

The 84,000-stupa figure is real and directly modeled on the legend of King Ashoka (阿育王), who was said to have built 84,000 stupas to distribute the Buddha's relics across the world — Qian Chu deliberately repeated that number. Chinese and secondary sources describe the small stupas as bronze (銅) for the 956 print run and iron (鐵) for the 965 print run; some later popular accounts describe them as gold, copper, or "fine steel" — these are not reliably distinguished across sources, so the script should stick to "bronze and iron" as the best-supported materials. Crucially, the 975 print run was not for these small Ashoka-style stupas — it was made for the Leifeng Pagoda itself, then under construction (Leifeng was begun in the early 970s and finished in 975). The 975 dharani scrolls turned up in the 1924 bricks, not in a small metal stupa.

Recommended wording:
> It's said 84,000 small stupas were made, copying the legend of King Ashoka — the 956 print went into bronze ones, the 965 print into iron ones. The 975 print wasn't for a small stupa at all. It went into the walls of a much bigger one: Leifeng Pagoda.

Sources:
- Kim/Gómez, *Religions* 12(1):13 (2021), https://doi.org/10.3390/rel12010013
- Dai Yue, "Secular Dimensions of the Aśoka Stūpa from the Changgan Monastery of the Song Dynasty," *Religions* 12(11):909 (2021), https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/12/11/909
- Baidu Baike, 一切如来心秘密全身舍利宝箧印陀罗尼经, https://baike.baidu.com/item/一切如来心秘密全身舍利宝箧印陀罗尼经/8855406

## 4. Leifeng Pagoda: built by Qian Chu, begun/completed 975, octagonal, storeys, brick with wooden eaves

**Verdict: needs rewording (dates)**

Leifeng Pagoda (雷峰塔) stood by West Lake in Hangzhou, commissioned by Qian Chu. Construction ran across the early-to-mid 970s (started roughly 972) and the pagoda is conventionally dated to 975, the year the dedicatory dharani was printed — so "975" is defensible as the completion/dedication year, but "built in 975" alone overstates precision; better to say "completed" or "dedicated" in 975. It was originally octagonal (八角) and five storeys (五層), with a brick body and wooden eaves, balconies, stairs and balustrades — the wood burned away over the centuries, leaving the brick core exposed before the 1924 collapse.

Recommended wording:
> Qian Chu built Leifeng Pagoda by West Lake, dedicated in 975 — an octagonal, five-storey tower with a brick body and wooden eaves and balconies.

Sources:
- Wikipedia, Leifeng Pagoda, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leifeng_Pagoda (cross-checked against museum/archaeology sources below; used for general structure only)
- Kim/Gómez, *Religions* 12(1):13 (2021), https://doi.org/10.3390/rel12010013
- 城市怎么办, "还原一个真实的南宋——雷峰塔重建工程篇," http://www.urbanchina.org/content/content_7184135.html

## 5. Collapsed 25 September 1924; rolled dharani copies dated 975 found in the bricks

**Verdict: confirmed**

Leifeng Pagoda collapsed on 25 September 1924, its brick core weakened by decades of villagers chipping bricks out (a folk belief held brick dust warded off illness/brought luck). Rolled paper scrolls of the 975 Baoqieyin dharani print were found stuffed into holes in the bricks — reportedly over 1,000 scrolls recovered from the ruins.

Recommended wording:
> Leifeng Pagoda collapsed on 25 September 1924. Rolled copies of the 975 dharani print turned up stuffed inside holes in its bricks.

Sources:
- Kim/Gómez, *Religions* 12(1):13 (2021), https://doi.org/10.3390/rel12010013

## 6. 2001 underground vault (地宮) excavation: gilt-silver Ashoka stupa in an iron case, containing a gold casket with a Buddha hair relic; dimensions and features

**Verdict: confirmed, with dimensions**

The Zhejiang Provincial Institute of Cultural Relics and Archaeology excavated Leifeng Pagoda's underground vault (地宮) beginning 11 March 2001. Inside an iron case (鐵函) was a gilt pure-silver Ashoka-type stupa (純銀阿育王塔, also described as 鎏金純銀阿育王塔), and inside the stupa was a small gold casket (金棺/金瓶) holding a strand of the Buddha's hair relic (佛螺髻髮). The dig recovered around 70–80 artifacts total from the vault (accounts vary: c. 70–346 depending on whether every coin/fragment is counted separately). The silver stupa is now in the Zhejiang Provincial Museum.

Features, matching the standard Baoqieyin/Ashoka-stupa ("寶篋印塔") type:
- Single-tier stupa in three parts: base, square tapering body, spire.
- Square body with repoussé relief panels of jataka scenes (佛本生故事, e.g. the prince feeding a starving tigress) and scenes from the Buddha's life.
- Four "banana-leaf"/mahajvala corner horns (山花蕉葉) at the top of the body.
- A spire of five rings (相輪, five-tiered wheels) topped by a jewel/flame finial.

Dimensions: museum-published figures give an overall height of 35.6 cm and a base width of about 12.5 cm per side; other secondary reports round this to "about 36 cm" tall, and one widely repeated caption gives 33.5 cm total height with a 12 cm base edge — these smaller figures likely reflect the stupa before/after a 2001–2009 conservation repair (it was found badly crushed and only fully restored by 2009). Recommend citing the museum figure and flagging the range rather than picking one number as exact.

Recommended wording:
> In 2001, archaeologists opened Leifeng Pagoda's underground vault and found, inside an iron case, a gilt silver Ashoka-style stupa about 36 cm tall — its square body carved with scenes from the Buddha's past lives, four leaf-shaped horns at its corners, a spire of five rings. Inside it: a gold casket holding a single strand of the Buddha's hair.

Sources:
- Zhejiang Provincial Museum 3D collection record, 鎏金银阿育王塔, https://museum24h.com/3d/zhebo/50.html (35.6 cm height, 12.5 cm base)
- China News, 雷峰塔地宫阿育王塔轰动一时 历经千年完好无损, https://www.chinanews.com.cn/cul/2011/06-20/3121892.shtml
- Sina News caption (museum label text), https://www.sina.cn/news/detail/4768439464365995.html (33.5 cm height, 12 cm base; notes stupa was crushed flat when the pagoda fell, restored by 2009)
- Zhihu, 雷峰塔地宫出土的阿育王塔，塔身镂刻佛传故事, https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/96098959

## 7. Leifeng Pagoda as rebuilt in 2002: storeys, height, octagonal diameter

**Verdict: confirmed, with figures (rebuilt structure only — original storey height/diameter not separately documented in sources checked)**

The current pagoda is a 2002 reconstruction (design by Tsinghua University's School of Architecture), completed October 2002 over the excavated ruins (which remain visible as a protected site beneath the new structure). It is octagonal, five storeys, with a total height of 71.679 m: roughly a 9.7 m base/plinth, a c. 45 m five-storey body (with elevators), and a c. 16 m spire. The octagonal base has a diameter of about 28 m and a circumference of about 88 m. This is a modern steel-frame structure, not a brick rebuild, and is explicitly a reconstruction rather than a restoration of the original Song-era fabric — the script should not imply these are the original Five Dynasties dimensions.

Recommended wording:
> The Leifeng Pagoda standing today is a 2002 rebuild over the excavated ruins — octagonal, five storeys, about 72 m tall.

Sources:
- 城市怎么办, "还原一个真实的南宋——雷峰塔重建工程篇" (rebuild engineering account), http://www.urbanchina.org/content/content_7184135.html
- 腾讯新闻/新浪, 吐槽不断的新雷峰塔真不值得一看, https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20220425A0501F00
