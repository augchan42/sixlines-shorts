## What the timeline is doing

It is not a history of the book. It is a set of correspondences: one symbol system (six lines,
two values, 64 states) turning up wherever people built systems of symbols, machines that read
symbols, or tests of who is doing the reading. Its own Shannon entry says it: "what the two share
is structure, not lineage". Three threads run through it, and the entries sit where they cross.

### 1. Binary: the lines as bits

Fu Xi's yin and yang; Shao Yong's ordering of the 64, which is counting from 0 to 63; Bouvet
sends it to Leibniz (1701), who publishes binary arithmetic and points to the diagram (1703);
Shannon's bit (1948), six to a hexagram; Turing's machine (1936) and his 1950 paper; the 8-bit
machines, where 64 is a round number (the Commodore 64); 8-Bit Oracle.

Retro computing hooks:
- Six bits was a real character size: 6-bit character codes on 1950s-60s machines (check).
- A byte holds a hexagram with two bits spare.
- Crowley's Liber Trigrammaton (1907) has 27 three-line figures, yang, yin and a dot for the Tao:
  three values, not two, so 3^3. The Soviet Setun (1958) was a ternary computer (check). Crowley
  is the ternary branch of the binary story.
- McKenna's Timewave Zero ran as software on home computers (Peter Meyer's program, 1980s; check):
  the King Wen sequence as data.

### 2. Correspondence: the lines as a lookup table

The Western occult reading treats symbol systems as tables to be lined up: Crowley's 777 (1909)
puts the trigrams and hexagrams beside the Tree of Life, tarot, planets (check what it has);
his Thoth tarot (the brand already has a card on it); Jung's archetypes and the collective
unconscious; Hatcher's Matrix Translation, each Chinese word a node in a matrix of senses.

AI hook: a correspondence table is what an embedding is, done by a machine. 777 is a
hand-made vector space. Hatcher's matrix is a hand-made knowledge graph. Leibniz hoped for a
characteristica universalis, a symbol system you could calculate with, and thought the
diagram was a lost one (check how he put it).

### 3. Who is answering: the book as the other side of a test

This is where Turing belongs, and it ties the 1950s together:
- Jung's foreword (written 1949) asks the I Ching how it feels about the new edition, and reads
  the answer as the book speaking for itself (hexagram 50, The Cauldron; check lines and second
  question). He treats the book as someone to talk to.
- 1950: the Wilhelm/Baynes English edition comes out the same year as Turing's "Computing
  Machinery and Intelligence", which asks whether a machine can pass as someone to talk to
  (check both dates).
- 1962: in The Man in the High Castle, Juliana asks the book why it wrote the novel and gets
  61, Inner Truth (check). The book asked about itself again.
- 1968/1982: Do Androids Dream / Blade Runner, the Voight-Kampff test: a Turing test run the
  other way, to catch the machine. Our corridor's searchlight is Blade Runner's.
- Today: a model reads the book. The question has turned round: not "is the book a person" but
  "can a machine read the book well". 8-Bit Oracle and Six Lines answer by method: Wang Bi's
  reading, a situation and positions in it.

### The I Ching as context for AI

- A fixed symbol system with 64 states and a 3,000-year commentary on each: the Ten Wings,
  Wang Bi, Cheng Yi, Zhu Xi, Wilhelm. The commentary tradition is the context a model reads
  the hexagram in; the hexagram is a six-bit key into it.
- The structure is checkable where an LLM's guesses are not: lines, trigrams, nuclear
  hexagrams, the changing line's neighbour on the cube (the cube short). A model's reading
  can be held to it.
- A hexagram and a changing line are a state and a transition, as in Turing's machine: read one
  line, change it, go to the next state (structure, not lineage).

## How it could be cut

A. One long timeline, every entry, about 2 minutes, the fly-through with a stop per date.
B. Three corridors, one per thread, each 45-60 s, sharing the Blender world: Binary (Fu Xi,
   Shao Yong, Bouvet, Leibniz, Shannon, Turing's machine, 8-bit, today), Correspondence
   (King Wen, Crowley, Wilhelm, Jung, Watts, McKenna, Hatcher), Who is answering (Jung 1949,
   Turing 1950, Dick 1962, Voight-Kampff 1968/82, today).

   others.

Recommendation: B, with the third thread first (C's order). It ties Turing in by his test,
not by binary alone, and it ends on the app.
